    Feel it in your body: 60 seconds

    1. Stand up, feet hip-width apart, knees soft. Close your eyes after a few rounds.
    2. Shift your weight from foot to foot, following the dot. Keep it small; someone watching should barely notice.
    3. Keep going for a minute. That slow, never-ending sway is what the crossing feels like from the seat you picked.
    leftright

    The dot follows the ship's sway at real speed: one full cycle every ~14 s.

    To make it easier

    • Sit in the middle of the ship, on a low passenger deck. That's where the ship moves least. The front, top lounges move the most.
    • Face forward and look out at the horizon. When your eyes see the motion your inner ear feels, you're less likely to get sick.
    • Don't read or scroll your phone for long stretches, especially in the middle of the gulf.
    • Eat something light beforehand. Skip greasy food and alcohol.
    • Seasickness tablets work best taken about an hour before you board. Ginger may help a little.
    • Outer decks may be closed in this wind. If you start to feel bad, close your eyes, lean your head back and breathe slowly.
